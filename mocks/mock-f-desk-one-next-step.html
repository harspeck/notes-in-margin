every action, when it shows, and what you need to take it

The desk shows one of these as the next step and queues the rest. The order below is the order the desk picks from: the first row whose "when" is true wins the top card.

ActionWhen the desk offers itWhat you need to know to take itWhere it leads
1Decide on insights A reflection has been connected to your questions and insights are undecided. This goes first because the conversation fades from memory fastest. How many insights, which reflection and conversation, how long ago, which questions they touch, and that nothing reaches your memo until you file. Review, then straight to a recommended next conversation.
2Reflect on what you heard You have highlights on a conversation and no reflection yet, and you stopped reading more than a few minutes ago or reached the end. How many highlights and stars, your last highlighted passage, and how long it takes (a few sentences is enough). The notebook, typed or spoken, then the AI connects it to your questions.
3Pick up where you left off A conversation is open and unfinished. Title and project, where you stopped and how much is left, highlights so far, and which of your questions it has spoken to. The reader at your saved place, audio at the same time.
4Draw themes from a question A question has about five or more filed insights from three or more conversations and no themes yet, or a teammate's matching question does. Insight count, how many conversations and people they come from, whether a teammate asked the same thing, and that suggested themes are only suggestions. Your codebook, or the team codebook when the question is shared.
5Read the next conversation Nothing is open and nothing is waiting. Also offered right after you file a review. A two-line summary, why this one (the question or line it serves), its length, and whether a teammate has already read it. The reader. "Not this one" brings the next pick, with no new AI call.
6Jot a question Always available in the side column. It becomes the top card only on a first visit, when you have none. That a question is scratch paper, not a plan, and two or three starting ideas drawn from the projects' conversation guides. Stays on the desk. The question starts gathering passages as you listen.
7Keep or drop an offered listening line The AI has offered lines and you have not decided. Shown in the side column, never as the top card. The line, the one-sentence reason, and that keeping it steers what gets recommended next. Stays on the desk.
8See what a teammate found A teammate filed something under a question like one of yours, or highlighted a conversation you have read. Who, what, which of your questions it relates to, and when. The Team page at that question, or the transcript at that passage.
9Summarise conversations Only when there is nothing to recommend because conversations have no summary yet. A setup chore, so it never competes with reading. How many are left, that it is a one-time step per conversation and shared with your team, and that you can stop it. Stays on the desk, then a recommendation appears.
One next step, not six lanesToday's desk shows every lane at equal weight, including empty ones. Here one card answers "what now", and the rest wait in order.
Every action carries its factsEach card shows the few facts that make the decision easy, so you never open a page to find out whether it was worth opening.
Questions always visibleYour questions stay in the side column with how widely each has been heard, because they are why you are listening.
AI offers stay smallAnything the AI wrote is dashed and marked with the sparkle. It never takes the top card away from your own unfinished work.
